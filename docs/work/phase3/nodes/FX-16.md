### Decisions (author)

1. Charset designation: `ESC (` or `ESC )` followed by one character that is not `\n`, `\r` or `ESC` removes the `ESC`, the `(`/`)` and that character (3 bytes for an ASCII character; for a non-ASCII character, the whole character, so the cut stays on a UTF-8 boundary).
2. `ESC (` or `ESC )` at the text end, or before `\n` or `\r`, removes only `ESC (`/`ESC )` (an escape never takes `\n` or `\r`, OS-12 Decision 2). Before `ESC`, it also removes only `ESC (`/`ESC )`, and that `ESC` starts its own sequence (same reading as the lone-`ESC` rule; nothing of the next sequence stays as residue).
3. `ESC` followed by `ESC` removes only the first `ESC` (1 byte); the loop in `strip_escapes` then reads the second `ESC` as the start of its own sequence.
4. No signature changes: `fn esc_len(text: &str, i: usize, osc_stop_cache: &mut Option<usize>) -> usize` gets two new match arms before `Some(_)`.
